# Chapter 2: Low Tide

_Sample text written by Claude Code._

The sea went out the way a crowd leaves a theater: slowly, then all at once, leaving the flats shining and strewn with things nobody had meant to lose. Tam grounded the boat at the end of the deep channel and they walked the rest, Mara's boots sucking at the mud, her toolroll heavy on her back.

The drowned gate stood a mile out, an arch of black iron up to its shoulders in silt. A lantern hung from its crown. Beneath the lantern sat a woman in an oilskin coat, writing in a ledger as if she had all night, while a boy of about twelve held an umbrella over the page. It wasn't raining.

"Late," said the woman, without looking up. "The bell is in an hour and forty minutes. I'm Ines Hale, and I keep this gate. The boy is Pell. He keeps the tide tables, and me."

"Mara Venn."

"I know. You're in the ledger." Ines turned back a few pages and tilted the book toward the light. "Venn, the third night of the flood. One key, silver, passed through. One man, not passed back."

Mara's mouth went dry.

"Gate law is simple," Ines said, and closed the ledger on her finger. "A key is spent when it opens. The lock is not. Your father's key is a very handsome piece of silver, and it is finished."

"Then why am I here?"

Ines looked past her, at the ferryman, and for the first time something in her face gave.

"Ask him," she said.
