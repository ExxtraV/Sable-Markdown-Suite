# Chapter 3: The Gate Opens

_Sample text written by Claude Code._

Tam had brought a blank.

He took it from inside his coat, wrapped in a scrap of sailcloth: a plain brass key, uncut, the same length and weight as the silver one. "A key is spent," he said. "A lock isn't. Nobody ever said a lock can't be asked a different way."

Mara turned it over. "You want me to cut a brother."

"I want the locksmith to cut a brother," said Tam. "That's what the debt is for."

She worked kneeling in the silt, Pell holding the lantern close, the old silver key clamped in a vise she carried for less desperate jobs. She took the shape from its teeth and the lock's memory from its keyway, felt for the wards with a pick no thicker than a whisker, and cut: one file stroke, then another, brass dust bright in the lamplight. Nobody spoke. The water had begun, very quietly, to come back.

* * *

The bell rang once across the flats.

"Now," said Ines, and her voice had lost its ledger flatness.

The new key slid in and stopped, and for a moment Mara was nine years old in the cold dark, feeling her father's hand let go. Then it turned. The gate did not creak. It simply stopped being a wall.

Beyond it, stairs went down into dry stone, and far below, small and steady, a lamp was burning.

"Someone's been keeping that lit," said Pell.

Nobody asked who. Mara picked up her toolroll, closed her hand around the brass key at last, and went down.
