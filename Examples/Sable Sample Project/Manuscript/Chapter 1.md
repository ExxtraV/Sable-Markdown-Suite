# Chapter 1: The Crossing

_Sample text written by Claude Code._

Mara Venn held the silver key flat against her palm and did not close her fingers around it. *Not yet*, she thought. Behind the boat, the lamps of Northwatch were going out one street at a time, the way they did whenever the tide began to fall.

The ferryman stood at the stern with his pole across his shoulders, a long gray shape against a grayer sky, letting the current do most of the work. Tam Orrin never looked back at the city. He said it was bad for the steering.

"You've gone quiet," he said.

"I'm counting."

"Counting what?"

"What I owe you."

He laughed, a single note, like a bell struck once in a drowned church. "Nobody's finished that sum, locksmith. Least of all you."

It was true. Twenty years ago the lower town had gone under in a night, and a ferryman with a pole had pulled a girl and her father's toolbag out of the black water. He had asked for nothing then. A debt that isn't named at the time can be named at any time, and this evening, at the top of the harbor stairs, Tam had named it.

*Take me to the drowned gate. Bring the key. Be there before the bell.*

Mara knew the law as every child of the coast knew it. She had learned it with her letters.

> Every gate has a keeper.
> No key opens the same door twice.

"You know this key's been through already," she said. "Whatever it opens, it can't open that."

Tam shifted his grip on the pole. The water slid by, black and unhurried.

"I know," he said. "That's why I need you instead of it."
